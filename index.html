SPORTS ANALYTICS · TEAM OF 4

NFL draft & contract analytics.

6 of the top 7

positions for career length are linemen or tight ends (ANOVA, p < .0001). Skill-position players should negotiate for guaranteed money, not long-term bonuses.

Question
Do draft round, position, and signing team predict career length and guaranteed pay?
My role
One of four analysts on a shared Tableau workbook and presentation, with recommendations for players, teams, and agents.
Tools
Tableau · One-way ANOVA · Correlation · 2011–2022 draft data
More findings & method ↗
View full size ↗

Teams differ on guarantees

Average guaranteed amounts differ significantly by signing team (p = .0455). The Rams, Chargers, Browns, Eagles, and Chiefs spend the most.

Draft round matters, with exceptions

First-round picks have longer careers on average (p < .0001). Quarterbacks and linebackers are the exception: second-round picks lasted longest.

Limits

Older draft classes had more seasons to accumulate games, so these are descriptive differences, not causal effects.

REAL ESTATE ANALYTICS · TEAM WAREHOUSE + INDIVIDUAL ANALYSIS

Crime, home values, and a county-level warehouse.

+$8,000

in average home value per reported crime reduced in Washington County (2020–2023). Benton, Saline, and Faulkner counties each topped +$1,000.

Question
Is crime reduction associated with rising home values across Arkansas counties?
My role
Built the individual crime-vs-price analysis; co-built the team’s SQL Server warehouse.
Tools
SQL Server · Python (Census API) · Power BI · Dimensional modeling
The warehouse & method ↗
View full size ↗

The build

Four fact tables (property, crime, population, economics) joined through shared county and date dimensions. Data came from the Census API through Python and from 15 CSV sources.

The hard part

The sources didn’t share a grain. We started at property level and moved to county level so the comparisons would be valid.

Limits

Crime reporting is incomplete in some counties, and this is an association, not proof that less crime raises prices.

RETAIL PREDICTIVE ANALYTICS · TEAM OF 3

Deciding who a $7 sales call should go to.

54% → 22%

misclassification: gradient boosting beat the baseline so that about 4 in 5 campaign calls would reach customers likely to buy a membership.

Question
Which customers should a paid-call membership campaign target?
My role
Built 3 of the team’s 9 advanced models and contributed to preprocessing and the final recommendation.
Tools
SAS Enterprise Miner · Gradient boosting · Neural networks · Decision trees
Method & drivers ↗

What drove the prediction

Recency of last purchase, interest in accessories, and email opens and clicks. Customers who engage recently are the ones who convert.

Method

We reframed a 0–5 interest survey score as a likely/unlikely target, handled missing values, standardized inputs, and removed redundant total/component variables.

Limits

The model predicts survey interest, not observed purchases. The next step would be measuring real conversion before rollout.
